# V3.2.1 — Statistical Terminology & False Certification Audit

## 1. Executive Summary

In safety-critical computer vision and AI safety engineering, terminology precision is an ethical and scientific mandate. Overclaiming certainty—such as confusing an empirical calibration with a mathematical theorem, or describing a sample result as an absolute guarantee—creates a false sense of security that leads to catastrophic real-world failures.

This report establishes the results of the **Statistical Terminology & Executive Language Audit** for Safety Backfire AI V3.2.1, enforcing strict prohibitions against unproven certification claims.

## 3. Epistemic Taxonomy: Classification of Claims

To prevent epistemic conflation, all claims across the release are classified into six distinct categories:

1. **FACT:** Verifiable physical reality recorded directly from hardware sensors (e.g. `test.mp4` contains 426 physical frames; camera gantry height is measured at $4.5\,\text{m}$).
2. **DERIVED METRIC:** Deterministic mathematical computation executed over raw measurements without stochastic assumptions (e.g. Bounding Box IoU, Precision $= \frac{TP}{TP + FP}$, Frame processing latency $= 11.6\,\text{ms}$).
3. **STATISTICAL INFERENCE:** Probabilistic claims inferred from sample data with explicit confidence intervals (e.g. Wilson 95% Confidence Interval for Perception Recall is $[0.9248, 0.9644]$; Poisson 95% CI for False Event Rate is $[1.614, 22.871]\,\text{events/hr}$).
4. **ENGINEERING ASSUMPTION:** A modeled constraint accepted as operational ground truth within the ODD (e.g. Floor surface planarity slope $\le 1.5^\circ$; camera mounting deflection $\le \pm 0.35^\circ$).
5. **LIMITATION:** An established physical boundary where system accuracy degrades or fails (e.g. Far-field detector misses beyond $8.5\,\text{m}$ under monocular ray dilution).
6. **UNKNOWN:** Any unmeasured, unmodeled, or unverified parameter (e.g. Camera intrinsic distortion matrix on historical legacy clips; airborne chemical gas concentrations).

## 4. Uncertainty Semantics Reconciliation (`k = 2.24`)

In V3.2, certain sections described $k = 2.24$ as a "mathematical guarantee of 95% safety coverage."
- **Audit Finding:** The factor $k = 2.24$ corresponds to the 95th percentile of a standard 2D circular bivariate Gaussian distribution ($\sqrt{\chi^2_2(0.95)} \approx 2.448$, adjusted downward to $2.24$ for unilateral Euclidean clearance bounds).
- In real warehouses, physical footpoint noise exhibits heavy tails due to floor glare, motion blur, and non-Gaussian tracking errors.
- **Reconciled Wording:** The $0.9760$ ($97.60\%$) empirical coverage observed across $N = 500$ calibration trials is an **empirically validated statistical coverage rate under tested calibration conditions**, not an unconditioned mathematical certainty.
