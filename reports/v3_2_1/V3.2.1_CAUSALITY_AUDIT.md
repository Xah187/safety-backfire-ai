# V3.2.1 — Causality vs. Correlation: Empirical Audit Report

## 1. Executive Summary

This report delivers the official audit of the causal attribution engine in Safety Backfire AI V3.2.1. In observational warehouse analytics, failing to distinguish between **temporal correlation** and **causal impact** leads to false conclusions: facility managers mistake exogenous economic slowdowns for safety gains, or mislabel seasonal traffic surges as intervention backfires.

The audit verified that V3.2.1 strictly enforces the fundamental causal safety invariant:
$$\mathbf{TEMPORAL\_CORRELATION} \neq \mathbf{CAUSAL\_EFFECT}$$

---

## 2. Difference-in-Differences (DiD) Quasi-Experimental Design

To isolate true intervention effects from background operational noise, V3.2.1 implements a two-zone Difference-in-Differences estimator:
$$\hat{\delta}_{\text{DiD}} = \left(\bar{R}_{\text{post}}^{\text{treated}} - \bar{R}_{\text{pre}}^{\text{treated}}\right) - \left(\bar{R}_{\text{post}}^{\text{control}} - \bar{R}_{\text{pre}}^{\text{control}}\right)$$
where:
- $\bar{R}^{\text{treated}}$ is the average risk exposure in the warehouse zone receiving the intervention.
- $\bar{R}^{\text{control}}$ is the average risk exposure in an un-intervened, parallel warehouse sector.

### 3.1 Parallel-Trend Violation & Refusal Protocol
When exogenous variables (such as facility throughput changes or shift changes) shift control-zone risk by more than $15\%$, the parallel-trend assumption fails. The engine automatically suspends binary causal attribution and outputs:
$$\text{causality\_status} = \mathbf{INSUFFICIENT\_EVIDENCE}$$
This prevents the AI system from fabricating causal certainty under confounded conditions.
