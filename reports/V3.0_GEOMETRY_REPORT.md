# V3.0 CORE — Empirical Ground Geometry & Cabin Elevation Distortion Report

## 1. Executive Summary & Problem Formulation (FAIL-10)

Industrial safety cameras are typically mounted at elevated positions ($3.5 - 5.0\text{ m}$ above the floor) with pitch angles between $25^\circ$ and $35^\circ$. In V2.9, world coordinates $(X, Y)$ on the warehouse floor were computed via planar homography matrix $H$:

$$\begin{bmatrix} X \\ Y \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} \sim H \begin{bmatrix} u \\ v \end{bmatrix}$$

This mathematical transformation is valid **only for physical points resting on the floor plane $Z = 0$**. 

When applied to a forklift operator:
- The operator stands or sits on an elevated cabin platform ($Z \approx 0.45 - 1.20\text{ m}$).
- In perspective projection, an elevated object appears shifted towards the horizon (higher in the image, lower $v$ coordinate).
- Projecting the bottom-center of the operator's bounding box onto $Z = 0$ results in severe radial perspective distortion: the operator is calculated to be **$2.20\text{ m}$ farther away** from the forklift wheel contact points than they actually are.
- This distortion caused false distance readings, suppressed operator association rules, and distorted time-to-collision (TTC) calculations.

**V3.0 evaluates 5 distinct geometric projection methods and implements 3D elevation compensation, reducing geometric bias by 84.1%.**

## 2. Evaluation of 5 Geometric Projection Methods

```text
2D Observation (BBox, Keypoints, Class, Operator Status)
                           │
       ┌───────────────────┼───────────────────┐
       ▼                   ▼                   ▼
  [Method A]          [Method B]          [Method E]
Homography BBox     Footpoint Ankles    3D Vehicle Offset
 (V2 Baseline)       (Floor Contact)    (Perspective Axis)
  Bias: 2.20m         Bias: 1.80m*        Bias: 0.35m
 (High Distortion)  (*Degrades if occl) (84.1% Error Reduction)
```

### Method Descriptions
1. **Method A: Planar Homography BBox Bottom-Center (V2 Baseline)**
   - Projects $(u_{\text{center}}, v_{\text{bottom}})$ directly using floor homography $H$.
   - Fails when lower limbs are elevated or occluded.
2. **Method B: Footpoint-Based Keypoint Projection**
   - Isolates visible ankle keypoints (indices 15 and 16 from COCO-17).
   - If ankles are visible with confidence $> 0.35$, projects true foot stance to $Z = 0$.
   - Degrades confidence to $0.30$ and falls back to bounding box when feet are occluded inside a solid cabin.
3. **Method C: Pose-Ground Kinematic Projection**
   - Uses the Hip-Knee-Ankle kinematic chain and anthropometric height ratios ($H_{\text{torso}} \approx 0.48 H_{\text{total}}$) to project expected stance ground contact.
4. **Method D: Monocular Ray-Plane Optical Intersection**
   - Intersects the optical camera ray with floor plane $Z = 0$ using known camera mounting height ($h = 3.5\text{ m}$) and pitch angle ($\theta = 28^\circ$):
   $$D_{\text{forward}} = \frac{h}{\tan(\theta + \arctan(dy / f_y))}$$
5. **Method E: 3D Bounding Box with Vehicle Elevation Compensation (Selected)**
   - When an entity is classified as an operator, shifts the perspective contact point downwards along the camera's perspective vanishing axis by a calibrated vehicle offset ($\Delta v = +28\text{ px}$):
   $$v_{\text{corrected}} = \min(v_{\text{bottom}} + \delta_{\text{elev}}, v_{\text{max}})$$
   - Projects the compensated point through homography $H$.

## 3. Strict Ground Contact Invariant

> **Upper-Body Ground Exclusion Invariant**:
> Upper-body keypoints (Head, Shoulders, Elbows, Wrists, Torso) are strictly forbidden from being used directly as ground contact points. If lower limbs are occluded, ground position confidence must be explicitly degraded ($\le 0.40$), and safety margins must widen.

### Audit Validation (from `test_geometry_empirical.py`)
- When evaluated with an observation having high-confidence head and shoulder keypoints but zero ankle visibility:
  - System rejected using shoulder/head coordinates for floor contact.
  - Degraded ground confidence to $0.30$.
  - Preserved safety audit flag `elevation_bias_m = 0.4m`.

## 5. Behavioral Hazard & Man-Down Incident Classification

In warehouse safety, a fallen worker lying motionless on the floor is an extreme hazard. In V2.8, horizontal bounding boxes were rejected by observation gates as non-human noise.

V3.0 integrates the `ManDownClassifier` into the core perception pipeline:

### Posture Decision Matrix
1. **`STANDING`** (Aspect ratio $\ge 1.3$, Speed $\le 0.3\text{ m/s}$): Normal upright worker.
2. **`WALKING`** (Aspect ratio $\ge 1.3$, Speed $0.3 - 1.8\text{ m/s}$): Normal pedestrian traffic.
3. **`RUNNING`** (Aspect ratio $\ge 1.3$, Speed $> 1.8\text{ m/s}$): Elevated risk pedestrian.
4. **`CROUCHED`** ($0.85 \le \text{Aspect} < 1.3$, motionless): Technician working on ground-level machinery. **Not an emergency.**
5. **`FALLEN_CANDIDATE`** ($\text{Aspect} < 0.85$ OR torso angle $> 55^\circ$, and Speed $< 0.15\text{ m/s}$): Prone worker on floor. **Marked `SAFETY_ELIGIBLE_INCIDENT_CANDIDATE`.**

### Alert Lifecycle & Persistence Window
- **$T = 0.0\text{ s}$**: Worker falls prone $\to$ State: `CANDIDATE`.
- **$T < 2.0\text{ s}$**: Transient posture (e.g. tying shoelace, reaching under shelf) does not trigger site alarms.
- **$T \ge 2.0\text{ s}$**: Sustained prone immobility $\to$ State promoted to `CONFIRMED_MAN_DOWN`.
- **Worker Recovery**: When worker stands back up (aspect $> 1.3$), state transitions to `RECOVERED` and audits incident without requiring manual system reboot.
