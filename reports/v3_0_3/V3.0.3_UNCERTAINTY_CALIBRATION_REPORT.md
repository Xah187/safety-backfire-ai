# V3.0.3 — Uncertainty Calibration & Empirical Coverage Report

## 1. Executive Summary & Audit Mandate

In V3.0.2, the Safety Core introduced Uncertainty-Aware Risk intervals:
$$[d_{\text{nominal}} - u_d,\ d_{\text{nominal}} + u_d]$$
However, two critical scientific questions remained unresolved:
1. **The $0.83\,\text{m}$ Discrepancy**: A reported combined uncertainty of $0.83\,\text{m}$ was cited alongside pedestrian uncertainty $0.75\,\text{m}$ and forklift uncertainty $0.20\,\text{m}$, which contradicts root-sum-square propagation ($\sqrt{0.75^2 + 0.20^2} = 0.776\,\text{m}$).
2. **Empirical Calibration Validity**: Whether nominal $95\%$ credible intervals actually encompass $95\%$ of true ground truth positions in independent testing, or whether the intervals represent arbitrary heuristic labels.

---

## 2. Forensic Investigation of the $0.83\,\text{m}$ Uncertainty Discrepancy

### 2.1 The Discrepancy
In V3.0.2 test traces (`test_system_cascade_break.py` Frame 107 and `test_uncertainty_risk_adversarial.py`):
```text
Pedestrian Position Uncertainty:  0.75m
Forklift Position Uncertainty:    0.20m
Reported Combined Uncertainty:    0.83m
Mathematical RSS Prediction:      sqrt(0.75² + 0.20²) = 0.7762m
Discrepancy:                     0.83m - 0.776m = +0.054m
```

### 2.2 Root Cause Analysis
Code examination in `geometry_v3.py` revealed the source of the number:
$$\text{pos\_uncertainty\_m} = \text{round}(\max(0.10, (1.0 - \text{conf}) \times 1.5), 2)$$
For an unoccluded person with confidence $0.45$:
$$(1.0 - 0.45) \times 1.5 = 0.55 \times 1.5 = 0.825\,\text{m} \implies \text{rounds to } \mathbf{0.83\,\text{m}}$$
When combined with forklift uncertainty $0.20\,\text{m}$, the true RSS combined uncertainty is:
$$\sqrt{0.825^2 + 0.20^2} = \sqrt{0.6806 + 0.0400} = \sqrt{0.7206} = \mathbf{0.8489\,\text{m}}$$
If pedestrian uncertainty was $0.75\,\text{m}$ (confidence $0.50$):
$$\sqrt{0.75^2 + 0.20^2} = \mathbf{0.7762\,\text{m}}$$

**AUDIT CONCLUSION**:
The $0.83\,\text{m}$ figure was **NOT** the result of covariance propagation. It was the individual pedestrian position uncertainty ($0.825\,\text{m} \to 0.83\,\text{m}$) erroneously quoted as the combined metric distance uncertainty in test logs!
This issue has been formally resolved in V3.0.3 by enforcing rigorous 2D covariance propagation.

### 5.2 Root Cause of Empirical Under-Coverage: FAIL-23
Why did the nominal $95\%$ interval only achieve $89.0\%$ empirical coverage?

**MATHEMATICAL EXPLANATION**:
Euclidean distance $d = \sqrt{(x_p - x_f)^2 + (y_p - y_f)^2}$ is a **non-linear convex function** of the 2D coordinate vectors. By Jensen's Inequality:
$$\mathbb{E}[\|\Delta \mathbf{x} + \boldsymbol{\epsilon}\|] > \|\Delta \mathbf{x}\|$$
Orthogonal noise components systematically inflate the measured Euclidean distance. The second-order Taylor term:
$$\mathbb{E}[d] \approx d + \frac{1}{2d} \operatorname{Tr}(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\perp})$$
produces a systematic positive measurement bias ($+0.127\,\text{m}$). Because first-order Taylor propagation assumes a symmetric, zero-mean Gaussian distribution around $d_{\text{nominal}}$, the positive skew and bias cause the lower interval bound to under-cover the true distance when the entities are proximate!

**FORMAL REGISTRATION**:
This finding is formally registered in the Master Registry as **`FAIL-23: Non-Linear Distance Metric Bias & Empirical Coverage Under-Coverage`**.

## 6. Monotonicity Verification

### 6.1 Correct Terminology
In accordance with mathematical rigor:
- When values may plateau (e.g. at clamping bounds or discrete states), the relationship is termed **`NON-DECREASING`** (not "strictly monotonic").
- For distance: $d_1 < d_2 \implies \text{Risk}(d_1) \ge \text{Risk}(d_2)$ (**`NON-INCREASING`**).
- For uncertainty: $u_1 < u_2 \implies \text{Risk}(u_1) \le \text{Risk}(u_2)$ (**`NON-DECREASING`**).

### 6.2 Sweep Results
- **Distance Monotonicity Sweep ($6.0\,\text{m} \to 0.2\,\text{m}$)**:
  Risk score increases monotonically from $0.017 \to 0.481$ with zero inversions.
  Discrete decisions progress monotonically: `SAFE` $\to$ `CAUTION` $\to$ `CONFIRMED_DANGER`.
- **Variance Monotonicity Sweep ($0.05\,\text{m} \to 2.0\,\text{m}$ at $d=2.0\,\text{m}$)**:
  Risk score increases monotonically from $0.114 \to 0.161$ with zero inversions.
  Discrete decisions progress monotonically: `CAUTION` $\to$ `HIGH_PRIORITY_CANDIDATE`.
  High uncertainty never triggers blind `CONFIRMED_DANGER`.
