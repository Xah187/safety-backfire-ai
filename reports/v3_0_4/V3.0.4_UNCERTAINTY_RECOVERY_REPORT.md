# V3.0.4 — Uncertainty Recovery & FAIL-23 Resolution Report

## 1. Executive Summary & Root Cause of FAIL-23

In V3.0.3, empirical coverage on 100 independent metric distance evaluation pairs was **$89.0\%$**, falling short of the nominal $95.0\%$ credible interval contract.
Diagnostic analysis revealed that **$100\%$ of missed cases fell below the lower interval bound** ($d_{\text{true}} < \text{lower}_{95}$), while zero cases exceeded the upper bound.

### 1.1 The Two Statistical Causes of Under-Coverage
1. **Jensen's Convexity Bias on Euclidean Norms**:
   The Euclidean distance function $d(\Delta x, \Delta y) = \sqrt{\Delta x^2 + \Delta y^2}$ is strictly convex.
   By Jensen's inequality:
   $$\mathbb{E}[d_{\text{obs}}] > d_{\text{true}}$$
   Specifically, perpendicular noise variance $\sigma_\perp^2$ inflates the expected observed distance:
   $$\mathbb{E}[d_{\text{obs}}] \approx d_{\text{true}} + \frac{\sigma_\perp^2}{2 d_{\text{true}}}$$
   In V3.0.3, this caused a systematic positive residual bias of $+0.1269\,\text{m}$.
   Because the first-order Taylor interval was centered at $d_{\text{obs}}$, the entire interval was shifted upwards, lifting the lower bound above $d_{\text{true}}$ for close-proximity targets ($d < 2.0\,\text{m}$).

2. **Dimensionality Mismatch (1D Gaussian vs 2D Rice Distribution)**:
   V3.0.3 applied the 1D Gaussian coverage factor $k = 1.96$ to the scalar distance.
   However, the Euclidean distance of a 2D bivariate Gaussian variable follows a **Rice distribution** (non-central chi distribution with 2 degrees of freedom).
   For 2 degrees of freedom, the $95\%$ probability contour requires an expansion factor of:
   $$k_{\text{Rice}, 95\%} \approx 2.24$$
   Using $k = 1.96$ truncated approximately $5\%$ of the distribution tail, inherently lowering coverage to $\sim 89-90\%$.

## 3. Mathematical Formulation of V3.0.4 Production Model (Method H)

In `GeometryEngineV3.compute_distance_with_uncertainty`:

### 3.1 Step 1: Second-Order Jensen Debiasing
Let $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\text{sum}} = \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_p + \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_f$.
Let unit vector $\mathbf{J} = \frac{\Delta \mathbf{x}}{\|\Delta \mathbf{x}\|}$.
Radial variance along line of sight:
$$\sigma_{\text{radial}}^2 = \mathbf{J} \, \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\text{sum}} \, \mathbf{J}^T$$
Perpendicular variance orthogonal to line of sight:
$$\sigma_\perp^2 = \operatorname{Tr}(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\text{sum}}) - \sigma_{\text{radial}}^2$$
The unbiased metric distance estimate is:
$$d_{\text{unbiased}} = \max\left(0, d_{\text{obs}} - \frac{\sigma_\perp^2}{2 \max(0.2, d_{\text{obs}})}\right)$$

### 3.2 Step 2: Calibrated 2D Rice Interval Expansion
For 95% expanded uncertainty under 2D bivariate error distributions:
$$k = 2.24$$
$$\text{lower}_{95} = \max(0.0, d_{\text{unbiased}} - k \cdot \sigma_{\text{radial}})$$
$$\text{upper}_{95} = d_{\text{unbiased}} + k \cdot \sigma_{\text{radial}}$$
