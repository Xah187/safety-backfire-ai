# V3.0.4 — Risk Regression & Safety Clearance Distinction Report

## 1. Executive Summary & Verification Mandate

A central question in safety perception systems is:
> **"Does the corrected geometry make physically correct decisions in the downstream safety engine?"**
> **"Can the system reliably distinguish a $0.8\,\text{m}$ critical clearance breach from a $2.0\,\text{m}$ warning buffer?"**

In V3.0.3, due to the $-3.974\,\text{m}$ Y-axis bias, physical distances on the longitudinal axis were collapsed by $>60\%$, rendering metric thresholding invalid.
This report validates that with V3.0.4's recovered geometry and calibrated uncertainty, safety decisions and clearance boundaries reflect true physical distances on the warehouse floor.

## 3. Stationary Proximity Hazard Preservation ($0.9\,\text{m}$ without TTC)

A critical failure mode in simple trajectory systems is treating proximity solely as a function of Time-to-Collision (TTC):
- When a forklift and pedestrian are stationary at $0.9\,\text{m}$ distance, relative velocity $v_{\text{rel}} = 0$, and TTC is infinite ($\text{TTC} \to \infty$).
- If risk logic requires active TTC convergence, a stationary worker standing adjacent to forklift tines is dangerously classified as `SAFE`.

### 3.1 Empirical Test Result
In V3.0.4, testing a stationary pair at true distance $d_{\text{true}} = 0.90\,\text{m}$:
- Estimated Distance: $0.882\,\text{m}$ (Error: $-0.018\,\text{m}$).
- TTC: `None` (Stationary).
- Proximity Hazard Flag: `True` ($d_{\text{est}} \le 1.5\,\text{m}$).
- Hazard Semantics: `CURRENT_PROXIMITY_HAZARD`.
- Safety Decision: **`CONFIRMED_DANGER`**.
- Reason: `direct_proximity_danger_with_high_quality_evidence`.

**Verdict**: Proximity hazard is strictly decoupled from kinematic convergence. Stationary hazards are preserved and never masked by infinite TTC.
