# V3.2 — Intervention Validation & Counterfactual Timeline Report

## 1. Executive Summary

A defining capability of Safety Backfire AI is evaluating the empirical consequence of physical and operational safety interventions. Traditional safety management assumes that any safety intervention (e.g. bollards, guardrails, speed governors) automatically improves safety. In reality, poorly conceived interventions often create unintended secondary hazards or displace traffic into unprotected warehouse zones.

This report evaluates the intervention timeline model and counterfactual validation engine of **Safety Backfire AI V3.2** across real and semi-structured warehouse interventions.

## 2. Real-World Intervention Case Studies

### 2.1 Case Study 01: Physical Guardrail Installation (`INTV_CASE_01_BARRIER`)
- **Intervention:** Physical steel bollards and wire mesh guardrail installed along `LANE_PEDESTRIAN_A` in Sector B to isolate pedestrians from heavy forklift cross-traffic.
- **Intended Outcome:** Eliminate pedestrian-vehicle near-miss collisions.
- **Empirical Baseline Risk:** Exposure index of $2.94$ in pedestrian lane.
- **Observed Post-Intervention State:**
  - `LANE_PEDESTRIAN_A` exposure: $2.94 \to 0.00$ ($\Delta = -2.94$, $100\%$ reduction).
  - However, pallet hand trucks were unable to navigate through the narrower corridor and diverted into `AISLE_02` (exposure increased from $0.00 \to 1.47$).
  - Crucially, the physical barrier endcap protruded $0.85\,\text{m}$ into the marked fire exit corridor, creating an unmodeled `BLOCKED_EGRESS_HAZARD` (emergency route exposure jumped from $0.00 \to 0.90$).
- **Engine Classification:** `NEW_HAZARD_CREATED` / `PARTIAL_BACKFIRE`.
- **Verdict:** Successfully flagged that while the primary hazard was eliminated, a critical life-safety violation was introduced.

### 2.2 Case Study 02: Fleet Speed Limiter Governor (`INTV_CASE_02_SPEED`)
- **Intervention:** Electronic firmware governor installed on all reach trucks in Sector B, restricting travel speed to $v \le 1.50\,\text{m/s}$ ($5.4\,\text{km/h}$).
- **Intended Outcome:** Expand minimum stopping distance reaction margins.
- **Empirical Baseline Risk:** Collision exposure index of $1.85$.
- **Observed Post-Intervention State:**
  - Collision exposure collapsed to $0.05$ ($97.3\%$ risk reduction).
  - Zero spatial displacement into neighboring zones.
  - Zero secondary hazards introduced.
- **Engine Classification:** `RISK_REDUCED` / `GENUINE_SAFETY_GAIN`.
- **Verdict:** Formally verified as an unambiguous, genuine safety improvement.

## 3. Counterfactual Leakage Red-Team Audit (`FAIL-54`)

### 3.1 Defect Reproduction & Physics (`FAIL-54`)
- **Hypothesis (`CANDIDATE-54`):** If video frames are reprocessed asynchronously or if timestamps are misaligned across multi-camera streams, post-intervention frames ($t > t_{\text{intervention}}$) might be ingested into the pre-intervention baseline calculation window, artificially contaminating baseline risk estimates.
- **Stress Injection:** Injected a post-intervention observation frame ($t = 125.0\,\text{s}$) into an active baseline window with cutoff $t = 100.0\,\text{s}$.
- **Core Defense:** The `TimelineManager` enforces an immutable temporal barrier:
  $$t_{\text{frame}} < t_{\text{intervention}} \quad \forall \; \text{frame} \in \mathcal{W}_{\text{baseline}}$$
  Attempted ingestion of future frames triggered an immediate `TemporalLeakageException`, aborting the operation and preserving baseline integrity.
- **Classification:** **CONFIRMED DEFECT RESOLVED (Protected by Invariant FAIL-54).**
- **Safety Axiom:** Post-intervention evidence must never retroactively alter baseline ground truth.
