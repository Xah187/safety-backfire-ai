# V3.2 — Operational Design Domain (ODD) Validation Report

## 1. Executive Summary

A core precept of safety engineering is that an AI system must possess explicit, mathematically audited awareness of its **Operational Design Domain (ODD)**. When operating inside ODD, the system guarantees correctness within specified statistical margins. When environmental or hardware conditions drift near or beyond ODD boundaries, the system must **explicitly degrade or refuse safety claims** rather than inventing false precision.

This report documents the empirical boundary audit of **Safety Backfire AI V3.2**, formalizing the physical conditions under which the system is certified, degraded, or out-of-domain.

## 3. Four-Tier Boundary Transition Policies

Under V3.2, every frame evaluation is tagged with exactly one of four operational states:

### 3.1 `VALID` (Normal Operation)
- All physical, optical, and temporal parameters are strictly within certified ranges.
- Full defensive clearance buffers ($k = 2.24$) and automated emergency braking/acoustic alarm dispatch active.

### 3.2 `DEGRADED` (Conservative Surveillance)
- Triggered by: Far-field distances ($8.0 - 10.0\,\text{m}$), low light ($50 - 150\,\text{lux}$), or network lag ($50 - 500\,\text{ms}$).
- Policy: Immediate emergency braking refused; advisory visual warnings dispatched; vehicle speed governor limits vehicle speed to $1.0\,\text{m/s}$.

### 3.3 `OUT_OF_DOMAIN` (Refusal to Assert)
- Triggered by: Ramp incline $> 3^\circ$, distances $> 10\,\text{m}$, total darkness, or latency $> 500\,\text{ms}$.
- Policy: Strict fail-closed protocol. System emits `INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE`. Zero false safety claims permitted.
- Safety Axiom: `OUT_OF_ODD != NORMAL_OPERATION`.

### 3.4 `UNKNOWN` (Epistemic Boundary)
- Triggered by: Corrupted sensor feeds or missing extrinsic calibration.
- Policy: Enforce maximum defensive exclusion envelopes (Rule: `UNKNOWN != SAFE`).
