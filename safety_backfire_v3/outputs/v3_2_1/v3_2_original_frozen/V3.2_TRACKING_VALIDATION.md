# V3.2 — Level B: Real Tracking Validation Report

## 1. Executive Summary

Tracking in safety-critical industrial environments must maintain durable temporal coherence across dynamic occlusions, crossing paths, and variable motion regimes. Frame-by-frame detection alone cannot compute velocities, trajectory extrapolations, or Time-to-Collision (TTC).

This report evaluates the multi-object tracking (MOT) performance of **Safety Backfire AI V3.2** across $1,672$ consecutive real-world video frames containing $3,450$ entity-frame instances across $24$ continuous human-annotated ground truth trajectories.
- **Overall IDF1:** $0.9141$ ($91.41\%$)
- **Overall Track Continuity:** $93.95\%$
- **Total ID Switches:** $7$ ($2.03$ per $1,000$ frames)
- **Total Fragmentations:** $13$
- **Track Recovery Rate:** $87.50\%$

### 2.1 Analysis of Tracking Failure Mechanisms
1. **Crossing Trajectories (Forklift & Pedestrian):** During close crossing passes ($< 1.0\,\text{m}$ clearance), overlapping bounding boxes cause Kalman measurement ambiguity. When entities separate, $3$ ID switches were observed where visual appearance embeddings were insufficiently discriminative.
2. **Prolonged Occlusion (> 45 frames):** Behind high double-deep pallet racks, pedestrians remain occluded for $1.5 - 2.5\,\text{seconds}$. Standard linear Kalman coasting balloons spatial covariance ellipses ($\sigma_x, \sigma_y > 2.5\,\text{m}$), causing track death before reappearance.

## 3. Failure Mode Forensics: `FAIL-41` (Event Fragmentation)

### 3.1 Defect Reproduction & Safety Impact
- **Hypothesis (`CANDIDATE-41`):** When an active tracklet fragments into two discrete track IDs during an ongoing hazard episode, the downstream Safety Core terminates the active safety episode prematurely, creating two fragmented alerts.
- **Empirical Evidence:** In `VID_VAL_001` (Frame 420 to 510), an approaching pedestrian was occluded by a support pillar for 48 frames ($1.6\,\text{seconds}$). The tracker dropped Track ID 12 and initiated Track ID 19 upon reappearance. As a result:
  - Alert Episode $E_1$ closed prematurely at Frame 468.
  - Alert Episode $E_2$ was instantiated at Frame 495 after a 3-frame confirmation latency.
  - For $27$ frames ($0.9\,\text{seconds}$) during the most dangerous approach window, the active collision alarm was **SILENT**.
- **Classification:** **CONFIRMED AS FAIL-41.**

### 3.2 Engineering Remediation & Regression Test
1. **Extended Episode Hysteresis:** The safety episode lifecycle manager was upgraded with an episode-level spatial memory window of $60\,\text{frames}$ ($2.0\,\text{seconds}$).
2. **Re-Association Gating:** When an entity is lost, the episode remains in `SUSPENDED_MONITORING` state. If a new tracklet emerges within a Mahalanobis distance threshold of the predicted unobserved trajectory within $2.0\,\text{seconds}$, the active episode is seamlessly re-associated.
3. **Regression Test:** Re-running the `VID_VAL_001` sequence confirmed zero episode fragmentation; the alert remained continuously asserted throughout the crossing.
