## 3. Capability Status Definitions & Operational Governance

Under V3.2 governance rules, capability status is strictly enforced:

1. **`REAL_WORLD_VALIDATED` (5 hazards):** Validated on continuous CCTV video under diverse warehouse traffic, lighting, and camera geometry. Suitable for operational advisory deployments.
2. **`LIMITED_REAL_WORLD_VALIDATION` (5 hazards):** Validated on controlled staged video clips or specific facility layouts, but requires multi-view camera installations or exhibits sensitivity to subtle environmental variations (e.g. liquid spill sheen, load tilt $< 4^\circ$).
3. **`SYNTHETIC_ONLY` (1 hazard):** High-altitude pallet falling object drops cannot be ethically or safely staged in live operating facilities. Validated exclusively via physics-based synthetic simulations.
4. **`SENSOR_REQUIRED` / `OUT_OF_ODD`:** Hazards involving non-visual factors (e.g. airborne gas leaks, structural micro-fractures inside rack steel) are strictly classified as out-of-scope for passive monocular CCTV.

## 4. Failure Candidate Audits: `FAIL-49` and `CANDIDATE-50`

### 4.1 `FAIL-49`: Hazard Misattribution in Dense Staging Clutter
- **Hypothesis (`CANDIDATE-49`):** In congested staging zones where pallets, hand trucks, and pedestrians intermix, the safety engine misattributes a static obstacle as an imminent moving collision.
- **Empirical Evidence:** In `VID_TEST_002` (staging dock), a stationary pallet placed near the boundary of the forklift drive lane was classified as `COLLISION_HAZARD` with a turning forklift $3.2\,\text{m}$ away, rather than `BLOCKED_AISLE_HAZARD`.
- **Classification:** **CONFIRMED AS FAIL-49.**
- **Remediation:** Enforced spatial polygon containment gating in `UniversalRiskEngine`. Before assigning kinetic collision risk, the target object's velocity vector must exceed the movement threshold ($0.2\,\text{m/s}$); otherwise, static spatial obstruction rules govern.

### 4.2 `CANDIDATE-50`: Multi-Hazard Masking
- **Hypothesis (`CANDIDATE-50`):** In a complex scene with multiple concurrent hazards, an active low-priority relation (e.g. pedestrian walking along designated walkway) suppresses an independent high-priority hazard (forklift about to strike rack upright).
- **Red-Team Stress:** 20 composite multi-hazard scenarios were evaluated containing concurrent pedestrian-vehicle interactions, blocked exits, and unstable loads.
- **Empirical Finding:** In all $20 / 20$ trials ($100\%$), the `UniversalRiskEngine` evaluated all active hazard candidates independently and asserted the maximal risk score ($\text{risk} = \max_i R_i$). Zero hazard masking was observed.
- **Classification:** **NOT REPRODUCED (Robust Invariant Verified).**
