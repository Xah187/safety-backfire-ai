# V3.2 — Inter-Annotator Agreement & Ground Truth Reliability Audit

## 1. Executive Summary

Ground truth data cannot be treated as infallible without statistical auditing of inter-annotator agreement. In safety-critical computer vision, human annotators exhibit subtle subjective discrepancies, particularly in identifying partially occluded contours, pinpointing exact floor contact points on wheeled machinery, and classifying borderline hazard severity states.

**Safety Backfire AI V3.2** executes a dual-method statistical agreement audit:
1. **Categorical Agreement (Cohen's Kappa $\kappa$):** Applied to discrete ontology labels (Entity Classification and Hazard Classification).
2. **Continuous Geometric Agreement (MAE, RMSE, and Euclidean Distance):** Applied to continuous image and floor coordinates.

---

## 2. Statistical Agreement Methodology & Rationale

Applying categorical statistics (such as Cohen's Kappa) to continuous coordinates is mathematically invalid because continuous spatial coordinates have infinite states. Conversely, applying distance metrics to categorical hazards fails to capture chance-adjusted classification agreement.

### 2.1 Categorical Concordance: Cohen's Kappa ($\kappa$)
For discrete classifications between Annotator 1 ($A_1$) and Annotator 2 ($A_2$), Cohen's Kappa is formulated as:
$$\kappa = \frac{P_o - P_e}{1 - P_e}$$
where $P_o$ is the observed proportional agreement, and $P_e$ is the hypothetical probability of chance agreement based on marginal class distributions.
- $\kappa > 0.90$: Almost perfect agreement; suitable for safety-critical ground truth baseline.
- $0.80 \le \kappa \le 0.90$: Substantial agreement; acceptable with secondary audit review.
- $\kappa < 0.80$: Rejected; annotation instructions ambiguous, requiring re-annotation.

### 2.2 Continuous Coordinate Concordance (MAE / RMSE)
For floor contact point $[u, v]$ and surveyed ground point $[X_w, Y_w]$ placement:
$$\text{MAE}_{\text{euclidean}} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{N} \sqrt{(x_{1,i} - x_{2,i})^2 + (y_{1,i} - y_{2,i})^2}$$
$$\text{RMSE} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{N} \left[(x_{1,i} - x_{2,i})^2 + (y_{1,i} - y_{2,i})^2\right]}$$

## 4. Disagreement Forensics & Preservation Policy

Out of 50 evaluated complex scenarios, 7 specific disagreements were identified between annotators:
1. **Occluded Pedestrian Boundary (2 cases):** Disagreement on bounding box extent when a pedestrian emerged from behind a high rack column ($A_1$ marked visible torso only; $A_2$ estimated full silhouette).
2. **Caution vs. Imminent Threat Boundary (3 cases):** Annotators disagreed on whether a forklift turning at $2.2\,\text{m}$ clearance constituted `HAZARD_PERSON_VEHICLE_NEAR` vs. `HAZARD_PERSON_VEHICLE_COLLISION`.
3. **Rack vs. Staged Pallet Clutter (2 cases):** Ambiguity over whether a damaged wooden pallet resting against a rack upright should be classified as a damaged `storage_rack` or an `unstable_load`.

### 4.1 Non-Destructive Resolution Policy
Under V3.2 data integrity rules, these 7 cases were NOT resolved by forcing an arbitrary majority vote or averaging bounding boxes. Instead:
- They are permanently flagged as `AMBIGUOUS_CASE` in the ground truth manifest.
- The evaluation engine tests against the union of valid interpretations, ensuring the AI model is penalized neither for conservative caution nor for adhering to alternative valid safety interpretations.
